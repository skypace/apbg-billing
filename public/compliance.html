Read this before you print the board

  • Which IWC Wage Order applies is still open. Order 1 (Manufacturing) indexes "Bottling (soft drinks)"; Order 7 (Mercantile) indexes "Wholesale houses"; Order 9 (Transportation) covers firms in the business of transport/warehousing for others — our own delivery drivers don't by themselves pull us in. DIR's test is the principal purpose of the business, and only one order applies unless operations are separately managed at every level. Confirm with DLSE before printing one.
  • Whistleblowers Are Protected must be on 8.5 × 14 legal paper with margins no wider than half an inch, so the type exceeds 14 point. A letter-size printout is non-compliant.
  • Form 300A posts February 1 – April 30 covering the prior year, even with zero injuries, and must be mailed or handed to route drivers and field techs who don't report weekly to the posting location.
  • "California Workplace — Know Your Rights" (SB 294) is a distribution duty. Every employee gets it by Feb 1 each year, in the language we normally use with them. Up to $500 per employee if we miss it.
  • Fill-in posters count only when filled in — Payday Notice, S-500 Emergency Phone Numbers, DWC-7 claims administrator, and the workers' comp carrier notice.
  • SB 553 workplace violence has no poster. The duty is a written plan, a violent-incident log, training, and records — the plan must be easily accessible to employees at all times.
  • Time Off to Vote is seasonal — up about ten days before each statewide election (next: post by ~Oct 24, 2026 for the Nov 3 general).

Any inspection — how to run the visit

  1. At the gate: ask for credentials. Log name, agency, badge number, arrival time.
  2. Page the escort and notify Sky (415) 713-3318. Nobody walks the site unescorted.
  3. Seat them at the records first — open Inspector Mode on the tablet and let them search. Volunteering the digital setup reframes it as a strength.
  4. Answer what is asked. "I'll confirm that and get back to you" is a complete answer. Don't narrate history or speculate.
  5. Never guess a quantity — pull the number up in the app.
  6. Photograph what they photograph. Write down each flagged item in their own words, in the moment.
  7. Correct trivially fixable things on the spot — an open container gets closed while they watch. Never move or conceal anything.
  8. Never sign an admission of fault. Signing receipt of a report is fine.

Before they leave — ask these four

  1. Which findings are violations and which are observations?
  2. Is anything classified serious / Class I, and on what basis?
  3. What is the correction deadline, and what proof of correction do you want?
  4. Will there be a reinspection, or is documentary proof sufficient?
