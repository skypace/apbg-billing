## 1. Your rights, our commitments

- You have the right to a safe workplace (Cal/OSHA, Title 8 CCR; federal OSHA Act §5(a)(1)).
- You have the right to see the **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** for any chemical you work with, at any
  time, without asking permission — the library is digital and searchable (§4).
- You have the right to report a hazard or injury **without retaliation** — to your supervisor, the
  EH&S Lead, or Cal/OSHA directly (1-833-579-0927).
- You may refuse work you reasonably believe is imminently dangerous while it gets evaluated.
- We commit to: fixing reported hazards, providing PPE at no cost to you, training before you're
  assigned a task with a hazard in it, and never asking you to hide anything from an inspector.

**If you don't know how to do something safely, stop and ask.** Nobody at this company has ever
been in trouble for asking. People get hurt guessing.

## 3. CO₂ safety — our number-one hazard

We store and fill carbon dioxide. CO₂ is invisible, odorless, heavier than air, and a **simple
asphyxiant**: in an enclosed or low-lying space it displaces oxygen and can make you unconscious in
seconds, with no warning smell. Treat it with the respect you'd give a live wire.

- The **6-ton bulk tank** (outside, area #2300) is managed by Airgas. Its monitoring alarm has a
  cellular backup. **If the alarm sounds: do not investigate — evacuate upwind and call the
  Emergency Coordinator.** Re-entry only after ventilation and an all-clear.
- **Never enter a walk-in, cooler, pit, or enclosed room where a CO₂ leak is suspected.** People die
  trying to rescue a downed coworker — the same gas that dropped them will drop you. Call 911;
  responders have air supplies.
- Liquid CO₂ and cold gas cause **frostbite** — never let it contact skin; wear gloves and eye
  protection when connecting or filling.
- **Cylinder handling:** upright and secured (chained or racked) at all times; valve caps on
  whenever a cylinder is not connected; move cylinders with a cart, never rolled or dragged by the
  valve; full and empty cylinders segregated and labeled.
- **In vehicles:** cylinders secured upright, valve closed, cap on, with ventilation. Never leave a
  cylinder loose in an enclosed cab.
- Only trained fill-station operators fill cylinders, outside, at area #2300, following the posted
  fill procedure.

## 4. Hazard communication — chemicals and SDS

Every hazardous chemical on site has a **Safety Data Sheet** in our digital SDS library (searchable
index on the compliance terminal; offline copy on the tablet; paper binder as backup). Small
quantities count too — a single spray can has an SDS.

- **Read the label before you use anything.** GHS pictograms mean: flame = flammable; corrosion =
  burns skin/eyes; exclamation = irritant; skull = toxic; health hazard (starburst torso) =
  long-term harm; gas cylinder = under pressure.
- **Never use an unlabeled container, and never decant into an unlabeled one.** If you pour it, you
  label it (product name + hazard).
- **Never mix cleaning chemicals.** Specifically, at this facility:
  - **Bleach (Clorox) + anything acidic or ammonia = toxic chlorine gas.** Our ice-machine cleaners
    (Manitowoc, Super-Kool) are acids — keep them and bleach strictly apart, in use and in storage.
  - **Chlorinating tablets (Kem-Tek) are a strong oxidizer.** Store dry, in their own container,
    away from every liquid chemical — moisture or acid contact releases chlorine gas.
- Solvent cements, primers, and spray paints are **flammable** — use with ventilation, away from
  ignition sources; keep containers closed.
- Oily or solvent-soaked rags can **self-ignite** — they go in the closed metal rag can, never in a
  pile or an open trash bin.
- New chemical on site? Tell the EH&S Lead the same week so its SDS gets filed **before** it goes on
  a shelf. If a purchase would push any material over 55 gal (liquid) / 500 lb (solid) / 200 cu ft
  (gas) on site, the EH&S Lead must know **first** — that quantity changes our CERS filing.

ask before starting.

## 6. Forklift / powered industrial trucks

Governed by Cal/OSHA 8 CCR §3650–3669 and federal 29 CFR 1910.178.

**⚠ Reading this handbook does NOT authorize you to operate the forklift.** Operators must complete
formal training — classroom + hands-on + a practical evaluation on our equipment — be certified by
the employer, and be re-evaluated **at least every 3 years**, after any incident or near-miss, and
when assigned a different truck type. No certification on file = no keys. Period.

Rules everyone follows (operator or not):

- **Pedestrians:** the forklift always has the right of way in the warehouse — not because it's
  polite, because it can't stop. Make eye contact with the operator before crossing its path. Never
  walk under raised forks, loaded or empty.
- **No riders. Ever.** No passengers on the truck, no people lifted on forks or pallets.

Rules for certified operators:

- **Pre-operation inspection every shift**, on the checklist: forks, mast, chains, hydraulics
  (leaks), tires, horn, lights, brakes, seatbelt, capacity plate legible. A truck that fails
  inspection is tagged out — key pulled, supervisor told. Driving a known-defective truck is a
  fireable offense because it's how people die.
- **Seatbelt on** before the truck moves. Tip-over protocol: stay in the seat, brace, lean away
  from the fall — never jump.
- **Know your load:** within the capacity plate rating, centered, tilted back, forks low (~4–6
  inches) when traveling. Loads that block forward view → travel in reverse.
- Slow at corners and doorways; horn at blind intersections; three truck-lengths behind any other
  moving equipment.
- **No one stands or walks under raised forks** — including you. Lower forks flat to the floor when
  parked; key out; wheels chocked on any grade.
- Charging/fueling in the designated area only — ventilated, no smoking, no open flame; eyewash
  location known before touching a battery.
- Cylinders, kegs, and equipment move on proper attachments or secured pallets — never loose on
  bare forks.

## 7. Warehouse, lifting, ladders, housekeeping

- **Lift with your legs**, load close to your body, no twisting mid-lift. Over ~50 lb or awkward:
  get a second person, a hand truck, or the forklift. A back injury is permanent; asking for help
  takes ten seconds.
- **Ladders:** inspect before use; 3-point contact; never the top two rungs; don't lean — move the
  ladder. No metal ladders near electrical work.
- **Housekeeping is safety:** aisles and exits clear, cords and hoses out of walkways, spills wiped
  immediately or coned until they are, pallets stored flat, nothing stored within 18 inches of
  sprinkler heads or blocking extinguishers, eyewash, panels, or the CO₂ shutoff.
- **Pallet jacks:** load balanced and low; pull on flats, push down ramps (never downhill in front
  of a load); watch your feet and everyone else's — steel toes don't beat a loaded pallet.
- **Racking:** damaged racking gets reported, not "watched." Never climb racking.

## 8. Fire safety

- Extinguishers are mounted every 100 feet. **PASS: Pull, Aim (at the base), Squeeze, Sweep.**
- Fight a fire only if it's smaller than a trash can, you have a clear exit at your back, and the
  alarm has already been raised. Otherwise: evacuate. Equipment is insured; you aren't replaceable.
- The deluge/sprinkler system and alarm stations (main office) are never blocked or disabled.
- Flammables (solvents, cements, aerosols) stored closed, away from ignition sources and the
  charging area.

## 9. Waste: used oil, universal waste, and what never goes down a drain

- **Used oil** (compressor oil from cooler dismantling, vacuum-pump oil from refrigerant service)
  is **California hazardous waste at any quantity**. It goes ONLY into containers labeled
  **"Used Oil"** with the accumulation start date, kept closed, on secondary containment. Pickups
  only by our registered hauler — receipts go to the EH&S Lead the same day.
- **Universal waste** — fluorescent lamps, batteries, aerosol cans, e-waste — gets labeled with a
  start date and never held past one year.
- **Nothing goes into a floor drain, storm drain, or sanitary sewer** — no oil, no glycol, no
  cleaning chemicals, no "it's mostly water." Alameda's storm drains reach the Bay. If you're not
  sure, it doesn't go down; ask.
- Spill? Small and you're trained: contain with absorbent, ventilate, tell your supervisor, used
  absorbent goes in labeled hazardous-waste storage — not the dumpster. Big, or CO₂, or you're not
  sure: evacuate the area and escalate (§2).

## 10. Refrigerants and equipment dismantling

- Venting refrigerant is a federal crime (Clean Air Act §608) — **recovery only, by an EPA
  608-certified technician**, with recovery equipment. Company certification on file:
  PA620014460.
- Before cutting into ANY sealed cooling system (cooler dismantling, scrap prep): **check the
  nameplate refrigerant.** CO₂/R744 systems are exempt from the venting ban; R134a, R290, R600a and
  the rest are NOT — those get recovered first, logged, and the compressor oil drained into the
  Used Oil container (§9).
- Dismantling work follows the posted procedure: de-energize, verify, refrigerant recovered, oil
  drained, then cut. Lockout applies (§11).

## 11. Equipment service, lockout, electrical & tools

- Before servicing anything with moving parts or stored energy: **unplug it or lock out the
  breaker, and verify it's dead** (try the start button). Cord-and-plug equipment: the plug stays
  under your control while you work.
- Never bypass a guard, interlock, or pressure relief. Never work on a pressurized line.
- **Electrical:** damaged cords and cracked plugs get tagged out, not taped; extension cords are
  temporary, never through doorways or under mats, never daisy-chained; keep panels clear (36");
  wet hands/wet floors and electricity don't mix — dry first; GFCI-protected outlets for anything
  used near water. Only qualified people open equipment beyond the plug.
- **Power tools:** guards stay on; blades and bits sharp and rated for the material; eye protection
  always; unplug to change accessories. Compressed air is for tools and parts — never for dusting
  off clothing or skin, and never pointed at anyone.
- **Knives/box cutters:** cut away from your body, retract the blade the moment the cut ends,
  replace dull blades — dull blades cause more injuries than sharp ones.

## 12. Vehicles & driving

- Seatbelts always, everyone, every trip. **No phone in hand while driving** — pull over or let it
  ring; no texting at lights.
- Daily walk-around before first use: tires, lights, leaks, mirrors, and **cylinder securement**
  (upright, valve closed, cap on, ventilation — see §3).
- Back in when arriving where practical (backing out into unknown traffic is where incidents
  happen); use a spotter in tight spots.
- Adjust for conditions: rain doubles following distance. Fatigued or medicated in a way that
  impairs — say so and don't drive; that conversation is always safe to have.
- Any vehicle incident, however minor, is reported the same day (§16). Photos at the scene, no
  fault admissions, exchange info, call the office.

## 13. Heat illness prevention (Cal/OSHA §3395)

Warehouse work in summer, outdoor CO₂ fills, and delivery routes all count.

- **Water, rest, shade:** drink water often (a cup every 15–20 min in heat — don't wait for
  thirst); rest breaks in shade or the cooled office whenever you need them, no permission needed.
- Know the signs: heavy sweating, headache, dizziness, cramps = **heat exhaustion** — stop, shade,
  water, tell someone. Confusion, no sweating, hot red skin = **heat stroke** — call 911, cool them
  aggressively, do not leave them alone.
- New to the heat (first week, or a heat wave)? You acclimatize gradually — pace yourself and say
  so; supervisors schedule accordingly.
- Never leave anyone alone in a hot enclosed space (truck cabs, containers).

## 14. Earthquake & natural events

- **During shaking: drop, cover, hold on.** Away from racking, stacked kegs, and cylinder storage —
  the injury risk here is falling objects, not the ground.
- **After shaking stops:** evacuate to the assembly areas (§2) and get counted. Do NOT walk the
  warehouse to "check things" first.
- The CO₂ tank and cylinder racks are our earthquake-vulnerable systems (ERCP §H) — after any
  significant quake they get inspected by the Emergency Coordinator before anyone works near them.
  If you hear the tank alarm or hissing, that's a §3 evacuation, upwind.
- Power outage: emergency lighting leads to exits; forklift and fill operations stop until power
  and the CO₂ monitor are back.

## 15. Workplace violence & security (SB 553)

California requires a Workplace Violence Prevention Plan, and this is your part of it:

- Threats, intimidation, or violence — from anyone: coworker, customer, delivery driver, stranger —
  get reported to your supervisor or the EH&S Lead immediately. Like injuries, reports are
  retaliation-protected, and "I wasn't sure it was serious enough" is exactly what we want reported.
- An angry or erratic person on site: don't engage or block them — create distance, get to another
  area, alert a supervisor; 911 for anything physical or any weapon.
- Doors and gates stay controlled: don't badge in strangers, don't leave the warehouse open and
  unattended. Visitors are escorted (§17).
- After any incident we log it, investigate it, and adjust the plan — same near-miss philosophy
  as §16.

## 16. Injury, illness, and near-miss reporting — and where to get treated

- **Every injury gets reported the same day** — including "it's nothing" cuts and tweaks. Late
  reporting hurts your workers'-comp claim and hides hazards.
- **Where you go for treatment:**
  - **Life- or limb-threatening → 9-1-1** (nearest ER: Alameda Hospital, 2070 Clinton Ave).
  - **Everything else work-related → Concentra in Oakland** — our occupational clinic:
    downtown **384 Embarcadero W, (510) 465-9565** (Mon–Fri 8–5) or airport
    **333 Hegenberger Rd Ste 100, (510) 638-0701**. Tell your supervisor first so the visit is
    logged and the workers'-comp claim form (DWC-1) goes with you — we provide it within one
    working day of learning about the injury.
  - First aid on site: kits in the inner hallway; eyewash in the main bathroom (chemical in the
    eye = 15 minutes of flushing, then Concentra or the ER).
- Serious injury or hospitalization: the EH&S Lead must know immediately — Cal/OSHA requires our
  report within 8 hours.
- **Near-misses are free lessons.** The pallet that almost fell teaches the same thing as the one
  that did, without the ambulance. Report them; nobody gets blamed for a near-miss report.
