## 3. Issuing a certificate (someone asks us for proof)

Landlords, chain customers, and property managers will ask for a COI. **We never write one
ourselves** — the broker issues it.

**Send every request to `coi@ramriskgroup.com`.** Not to a carrier, not to an individual at the
brokerage — that inbox is the queue, and mail sent elsewhere waits behind someone's calendar. The
portal has a **Compose the request** button on *Compliance & Safety → Insurance & Company* that fills
in the four required fields for you.

**Ram Risk Group requires four things.** A request missing any of them comes back for more
information, which costs you a day:

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. Certificate holder** | Full legal name **and complete address**. The most common omission. |
| **2. Description of operations** | If their contract calls for one. |
| **3. Coverage types and limits** | Whatever their contract requires — check it against §1 *before* promising anything. |
| **4. Endorsement requirements** | Additional insured, waiver of subrogation, primary and non-contributory. State them explicitly. |

**Their hours are Monday–Friday, 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM**, and anything after hours is handled the next
business day. Their office is in Florida, so if those are Eastern hours their 5 PM is our 2 PM —
treat early afternoon as the cut-off. **A Friday-afternoon request for a Monday job start is a
Tuesday certificate.** Ask the day you learn a certificate is needed, not the day before it is.

**How to request one**

1. Get from the requester, in writing: the exact **legal name and address of the certificate holder**,
   and which of the three things they need:
   - **Certificate holder** — they just want a copy and to be notified. Costs nothing.
   - **Additional insured** — they want coverage under *our* policy for claims arising from our work.
     **Requires an endorsement to the policy.** A checkbox on a certificate does not do it.
   - **Waiver of subrogation** — our carrier gives up the right to recover from them. Also an
     endorsement.
   - Sometimes **primary and non-contributory** wording as well.
2. Email **coi@ramriskgroup.com** with the holder details and the exact requirement. (Auto and
   workers' comp are still placed elsewhere — see §1 — so a certificate that must show those lines
   may need Progressive or Embroker as well. Say up front which lines the contract requires.)
3. When the certificate comes back, **file it in the app** against the policy, and send it on.

> **The rule worth remembering:** a certificate is *evidence*, not coverage. If a contract requires
> additional-insured status, ask the broker for the **endorsement** and keep it with the certificate.
> The ACORD form says this in its own header, and it is the single most common misunderstanding in
> commercial insurance.

## 4. Collecting certificates (vendors and contractors)

**Every contractor and service vendor gives us a current COI before their first day of work.** No
COI, no work — this is not negotiable and it is not personal.

**Why it is worth being strict:** at the workers'-comp premium audit, **any uninsured subcontractor
gets charged to our policy as though they were our employee**. A missing certificate is not a filing
gap; it is an invoice arriving a year later.

**What to require**

| Coverage | Typical minimum to ask for |
|---|---|
| General liability | $1M each occurrence / $2M aggregate |
| Workers' compensation | Statutory, with employers' liability |
| Commercial auto | $1M CSL if they drive to our sites |
| Additional insured + waiver of subrogation | Where our agreement with them requires it |

**How to file it:** Compliance & Safety → **Vendors & Contractors**. Create the party first if they
are new, then attach the COI so the expiry date drives the reminder. Expired vendor COIs surface on
the same weekly digest as everything else.

## 5. Claims — what to do

### Vehicle collision

Follow the safety handbook **§C8** at the scene: stop, cones, people first, call the office, **never
admit fault**, photograph everything, collect witnesses. Then:

1. **Report to the office the same day**, however minor.
2. Office reports to **Progressive: 1-800-444-4487** (or the app/portal). Have the policy number
   `975506554`, the vehicle VIN, date/time/location, the other party's information, the police report
   number, and the photos.
3. **Remember the $3,500 deductible.** Damage below that is effectively self-insured — but *still
   report it*. Non-reported damage discovered later is far worse than a small claim, and a
   third-party injury can surface weeks after a "minor" impact.
4. Post-incident testing may apply per company policy.

### Employee injury

Follow the safety handbook **§G1**. In short: first aid → **Concentra Oakland** for anything
non-emergency, **911** for anything serious → tell the supervisor before driving anywhere → **we give
the employee a DWC-1 claim form within one working day** → report to AmTrust/Embroker → serious
injury, amputation, loss of an eye, or hospitalisation also goes to **Cal/OSHA at 1-833-579-0927
within 8 hours**.

Then: investigate (what changed, not who to blame), offer modified duty where the treating provider
allows it, and keep the employee in contact with us — the single biggest driver of claim cost is a
worker who feels forgotten.

### Damage or injury at a customer site

1. Make it safe. Get medical help if anyone is hurt.
2. Tell the customer's manager **and** your supervisor the same day, with photos.
3. **Do not accept liability, promise repairs, or agree to pay.** "I'll report this and someone will
   be in touch" is the complete answer.
4. Office notifies the GL broker (Thimble/Coterie) with the date, location, what happened, what was
   damaged, and who was present.

### Product problem

Anything suggesting a customer was made ill, or product left our control contaminated or
mislabelled: **stop distribution of the affected lot immediately**, tell Sky, and preserve the
product, the batch records, and the co-packer paperwork. Products–completed operations sits under the
GL at $2M aggregate. Do not discuss cause or fault with the customer beyond gathering facts.

## 7. Premium audit (workers' comp)

The carrier audits payroll after the policy year. Prepare by keeping, all year:

- Payroll by **class code** — office, warehouse, drivers, and service techs are different rates, and
  misclassification is expensive in both directions.
- **Certificates of insurance from every subcontractor** (§4). This is the big one.
- 1099s and subcontractor payment detail.
- Overtime records — the excess portion is often excludable.
- Officer inclusion elections. **Ours are currently included** (the COI shows officers *not*
  excluded), which affects premium.

Full checklist: Compliance & Safety → **Inspections → Workers' Comp**.

## 9. Hard rules

1. **No contractor works without a current COI on file.** It becomes our premium otherwise.
2. **Never state or imply fault** — at a collision, at a customer site, or in writing afterwards.
3. **Never let a policy lapse.** GL and auto expire 12/31; workers' comp 01/01. Bind in early December.
4. **Report everything the same day**, including damage below the deductible.
5. **A certificate is not coverage.** If a contract requires additional insured, get the endorsement.
6. **Tell the broker when the operation changes.** New equipment, a new service line, a new location,
   or a materially different headcount can all void assumptions the policy was priced on.
